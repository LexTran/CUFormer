CUFormer registers intraoperative US bone surfaces to preoperative CT for radiation-free orthopedic navigation. Tracked B-mode images are reconstructed into a partial point cloud and aligned with the CT model. CT-US results span the tibia, fibula, foot, and femur, and the spine example adds CT-RGB-D registration.

Abstract

Registering intraoperative Ultrasound (US) bone-surface point clouds to preoperative Computed Tomography (CT) models can support radiation-free orthopedic navigation. Differences in imaging physics produce distinct surface sampling patterns, complicating cross-modal feature matching. Bone strongly reflects ultrasound and casts an acoustic shadow, so US captures only the probe-facing bone surface and leaves most CT points unmatched. This partial coverage makes correspondences ambiguous on near-symmetric bones. We propose CUFormer, a modality-aware registration framework. We introduce a Modality-Aware Transformer (MAT) that encodes input modality through identity embeddings and updates features through modality-specific adapters within a shared geometric matcher. We model imaging visibility and use predicted reference-side visibility to weight coarse and fine correspondences. CUFormer achieves the lowest mean registration errors among evaluated methods on two CT-US datasets, UltraBones100k and UltraBonesHip, and the CT-RGB-D dataset SpineDepth. On UltraBones100k, it reaches mean rotation error 4.53°, translation error 3.19 mm, and Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) 2.54 mm, reducing RMSE by 65.8% relative to the best-performing baseline by this metric. These results demonstrate CUFormer's effectiveness in aligning partial bone surfaces with preoperative CT across anatomical sites.

Method

CUFormer follows a coarse-to-fine registration pipeline. A shared KPConv-FPN encoder extracts multi-resolution features from the CT reference and the US source. MAT supplies modality identity embeddings and modality-specific residual updates within the shared geometric matcher. Reference-side visibility weights the coarse scores before superpoint correspondence selection and biases the fine scores before Sinkhorn optimal transport establishes point correspondences. A local-to-global estimator then recovers the rigid transform.

Modality-Aware Transformer

Shared geometric features do not explicitly encode the acquisition modality. MAT adds two learnable embeddings, ect and eus, to the coarse features before the transformer and, through a projection φ, to the fine features before matching. The superpoint transformer contains three pairs of geometric self-attention (GeoSA) and cross-attention (CA) blocks. Separate CT and US gated bottleneck adapters follow each shared block, Adapter(x) = x + γ·WupReLU(Wdownx), with γ initialized to zero so that each adapter starts as an identity mapping. The attention layers stay shared, and only the embeddings and adapters are modality specific.

Reference-side visibility

US observes only a thin strip of the bone, so most CT points have no counterpart in the source. CUFormer models imaging visibility through geometric overlap and predicts which CT regions the US observes. The visibility head hierarchically fuses coarse, fine, and full-resolution CT features along the point-to-node hierarchy, and a small per-point network of 1×1 convolutions with group normalization and a sigmoid outputs the visibility probability vi.

Supervision uses continuous soft targets. For each CT point, the distance di to the ground-truth-aligned source is mapped to si = clip(1 − di/ρ, 0, 1). Points whose target lies in the uncertain band between the negative and positive thresholds are masked.

Visibility-guided matching

At the coarse level, each aggregated visibility becomes a weight w that scales the superpoint scores before global top-K selection, favoring visible CT regions. At the fine level, λf log w is added to each reference point's row of point-pair scores before Sinkhorn optimal transport, while the scores of the unmatched (dustbin) entries stay unchanged.

Two-stage training

Stage 1 initializes the shared parameters from GeoTransformer weights pretrained on 3DMatch and trains the matcher, including MAT, with a weighted circle loss on superpoint descriptors and the negative log-likelihood of ground-truth point correspondences. Stage 2 freezes the matcher and trains only the visibility head with a smooth-L1 loss against the soft targets.

On the CT-US datasets, CUFormer achieves the lowest mean errors and FR, with 4.53°, 3.19 mm, 2.54 mm, and FR 0.0% on UltraBones100k and 22.00°, 12.55 mm, 15.07 mm, and FR 1.5% on UltraBonesHip. On UltraBonesHip, where the hip anatomy is deeper and harder to image with US, errors increase for the three learning-based baselines with the lowest RMSE on UltraBones100k. CUFormer also degrades there but retains the lowest RMSE. On SpineDepth, CUFormer obtains 4.72°, 2.18 mm, 2.36 mm, and FR 0.0%. Several baselines also perform well on this dataset, and CUFormer still reduces RMSE by 25.3% relative to CoFiNet.

Each module improves Baseline on its own: MAT reduces RMSE from 13.56 to 5.41 mm, and Visibility reduces it to 7.88 mm. Without pretraining, RMSE rises from 5.41 to 16.60 mm, about 3.1× that of MAT, which indicates that initializing the shared parameters from same-modality pretraining is critical when paired CT-US data are limited. On top of MAT, visibility guidance reduces RMSE by 53.0%, from 5.41 to 2.54 mm, compared with 41.9% on Baseline, and combining both modules gives the best results.

Either stage alone yields most of the gain, and fusing visibility at both stages gives the lowest errors among the predicted-visibility configurations. The remaining gap to the GT-visibility oracle is 0.02°, 0.34 mm, and 0.19 mm, so fusion at both stages recovers 99.2%, 88.9%, and 93.8% of the oracle's improvement in RRE, RTE, and RMSE. On the test scans of one randomly selected UltraBones100k split, the visibility head reaches an Average Precision of 92.04%, an AUROC of 94.81%, an F1 of 86.33%, and an Expected Calibration Error of 0.025.

Robustness to initial misalignment

CUFormer has the lowest mean RMSE at every perturbation level in both sweeps, rising from 2.64 to 3.70 mm between 0° and 90° of rotation, and the lowest mean RRE except at 90°, where GeoTransformer reaches 8.47° and CUFormer 9.29°. The protocol and success and flip rates are given in Supplementary Section S2 of the paper.

Runtime

On NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPUs, the CUFormer forward pass takes 0.055 to 0.115 s per scan on average across the three datasets, while the complete per-case optimization of NBReg takes 13.9 to 23.4 s. The two timing scopes differ: CUFormer excludes CPU neighborhood construction and host-to-device transfer, and NBReg was measured on GPUs shared with other workloads. Details are given in Supplementary Section S1 of the paper.
